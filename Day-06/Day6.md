# 1. What Is Data Exploration?

Data exploration is the process of examining a dataset to understand its structure, identify patterns, compare values, and discover useful insights.

# Why Is Data Exploration Important?

- Helps understand the characteristics of a dataset.
- Makes it easier to identify high and low values.
- Helps answer business questions.
- Supports data-driven decision-making.
- Prepares data for reporting and visualization.
- Helps identify unusual patterns that require further investigation.

# 3. Sorting Data in Excel

### Definition

Sorting is the process of arranging records in a particular order based on the values in one or more columns.

### Types of Sorting

**Ascending Order**
- Numbers: Smallest to largest.
- Text: A to Z.
- Dates: Oldest to newest.

**Descending Order**
- Numbers: Largest to smallest.
- Text: Z to A.
- Dates: Newest to oldest.

### Example

Original revenue values:

`800, 25000, 60000, 1500`

After sorting in ascending order:

`800, 1500, 25000, 60000`

After sorting in descending order:

`60000, 25000, 1500, 800`

### Steps to Sort Data

1. Open the sales dataset in Excel.
2. Select a cell inside the dataset.
3. Go to the **Data** tab.
4. Select **Sort** or use the sorting options.
5. Choose the Revenue column.
6. Select **Largest to Smallest** or **Smallest to Largest**.
7. Review the sorted records.

**Important:** Sort the entire table together so that values remain associated with their correct records.

### Business Applications

- Identify the highest-revenue orders.
- Find the lowest-selling products.
- Arrange transactions by date.
- Compare sales performance.
- Identify unusually high or low values.

## 4. Filtering Data in Excel

### Definition

Filtering is the process of displaying only records that satisfy specific conditions while hiding the remaining records from the current view.

### Example

Suppose the manager wants to see only sales from Hyderabad.

Apply this condition:

`City = Hyderabad`

Result:

| Order ID | Product | City | Revenue (₹) |
|---|---|---|---:|
| 101 | Laptop | Hyderabad | 60000 |
| 103 | Phone | Hyderabad | 25000 |

The other cities are hidden from the filtered view. Their records are not deleted.

### Steps to Apply a Filter

1. Select a cell inside the dataset.
2. Go to **Data → Filter**.
3. Click the dropdown arrow in the required column.
4. Select the values or conditions you need.
5. Click OK if prompted.
6. Review the matching records.

**Shortcut:** `Ctrl + Shift + L` toggles Excel filters on or off.

### Types of Filters

#### A. Text Filters

Used to filter text columns.

Examples:
- City equals Hyderabad.
- Product contains Laptop.
- Category begins with Electronics.

#### B. Number Filters

Used to filter numerical columns.

Examples:
- Revenue greater than ₹10,000.
- Revenue less than ₹5,000.
- Revenue between ₹10,000 and ₹50,000.

#### C. Date Filters

Used to filter date columns.

Examples:
- Orders from a specific month.
- Orders after a selected date.
- Orders from a particular quarter.

### Applying Multiple Filters

Business question:

Show Electronics orders from Hyderabad with revenue greater than ₹30,000.

Conditions:

1. City = Hyderabad
2. Category = Electronics
3. Revenue > 30000

Only Order ID 101 satisfies all three conditions.

When filters are applied to multiple columns, the resulting records must normally satisfy each column's selected filter.

### Business Applications

- Analyze sales for a particular city.
- Find high-value orders.
- Review transactions from a specific period.
- Focus on particular product categories.
- Investigate records that satisfy multiple conditions.

## 5. Grouping Data

### Definition

Grouping means organizing records into categories to analyze or summarize them.

For example, instead of reviewing every sales transaction, a Data Analyst can group records by city and calculate the total revenue for each city.

### Example: Revenue by City

| City | Number of Orders | Total Revenue (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Hyderabad | 2 | 85000 |
| Chennai | 2 | 55800 |
| Bengaluru | 2 | 13500 |
| **Grand Total** | **6** | **154300** |

### Calculations

**Hyderabad Revenue**

`60000 + 25000 = ₹85000`

**Chennai Revenue**

`800 + 55000 = ₹55800`

**Bengaluru Revenue**

`1500 + 12000 = ₹13500`

**Overall Revenue**

`85000 + 55800 + 13500 = ₹154300`

### Types of Group Summaries

- **Sum:** Calculates the total value in each group.
- **Count:** Counts the number of records in each group.
- **Average:** Calculates the average value for each group.

### Business Applications

- Calculate total revenue by city.
- Compare sales across product categories.
- Count orders by region.
- Calculate average order revenue.
- Identify high-performing business segments.

## 6. PivotTables in Excel

### Definition

A PivotTable is an Excel tool used to summarize, group, aggregate, and analyze large datasets without manually writing a formula for every category.

### Steps to Create a PivotTable

1. Select a cell inside your sales dataset.
2. Go to **Insert → PivotTable**.
3. Confirm the table or data range.
4. Choose where to place the PivotTable.
5. Drag **City** into the Rows area.
6. Drag **Revenue** into the Values area.
7. Ensure the calculation is set to **Sum of Revenue**.
8. Review the summary.

### Example Output

| City | Sum of Revenue (₹) |
|---|---:|
| Bengaluru | 13500 |
| Chennai | 55800 |
| Hyderabad | 85000 |
| **Grand Total** | **154300** |

You can replace City with Category in the Rows area to analyze revenue by product category.

**Important:** Check the aggregation setting. Sum calculates total revenue, while Count calculates the number of values or records.

## 7. Important Excel Formulas

Assume the dataset uses these columns:

- Column A: Order ID
- Column B: Product
- Column C: City
- Column D: Category
- Column E: Revenue

### A. SUMIF

**Purpose:** Adds values that meet a specified condition.

Formula:

```excel
=SUMIF(C2:C7,"Hyderabad",E2:E7)
```

Explanation:
- `C2:C7` is the criteria range.
- `"Hyderabad"` is the condition.
- `E2:E7` is the range to sum.

Expected result: `85000`

### B. COUNTIF

**Purpose:** Counts cells that satisfy a condition.

Formula:

```excel
=COUNTIF(C2:C7,"Chennai")
```

Expected result: `2`

### C. AVERAGEIF

**Purpose:** Calculates the average of values that satisfy a condition.

Formula:

```excel
=AVERAGEIF(C2:C7,"Hyderabad",E2:E7)
```

Expected result: `42500`

### Formula Comparison

| Formula | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `SUMIF` | Sum matching values | Revenue by city |
| `COUNTIF` | Count matching records | Orders by city |
| `AVERAGEIF` | Average matching values | Average order revenue by city |

## 8. Sorting vs Filtering vs Grouping

| Feature | Sorting | Filtering | Grouping |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main purpose | Arrange records | Display matching records | Summarize records by category |
| Changes record order | Yes | Not necessarily | Not necessarily |
| Hides nonmatching records | No, normally | Yes | No, not by itself |
| Example | Highest revenue first | Only Hyderabad orders | Total revenue by city |
| Excel tool | Sort | AutoFilter | PivotTable and formulas |

These techniques serve different purposes and are often used together during data exploration.

## 9. Practical Business Scenario

Imagine working as a Data Analyst for an e-commerce company.

Your manager asks:

"Which cities generate the highest revenue, and which orders should we investigate?"

### Analysis Process

1. Sort the Revenue column from largest to smallest.
2. Filter orders above ₹10,000.
3. Group records by City.
4. Calculate total revenue by city using a PivotTable.
5. Count the orders in each city.
6. Compare the results and communicate your findings.

### Findings from the Sample Dataset

- Hyderabad generated the highest total revenue: ₹85,000.
- Chennai generated ₹55,800.
- Bengaluru generated ₹13,500.
- Order 101 had the highest individual order revenue: ₹60,000.
- All three cities had two orders each.

### Business Insight

Hyderabad generated the highest revenue in this sample despite having the same number of orders as the other cities. The higher total is driven by the value of its orders.

However, this small dataset alone does not explain why revenue differs between cities. Further analysis of customer behavior, product mix, pricing, and time periods would be required.
