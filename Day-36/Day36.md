# Day 36 – Power BI Visualizations

Power BI Visualizations are graphical representations of data that help users understand information easily.

Instead of analyzing thousands of rows and columns, we use charts, graphs, cards, and maps to identify patterns, trends, and business insights.

**Example:** A company can use a sales chart to identify which month generated the highest revenue.

## 2. Types of Power BI Visualizations

### 1. Card Visual
- Displays a single important numerical value.
- Used to show Key Performance Indicators (KPIs).
- Example: Total Sales = ₹5,00,000.
- Other examples: Total Profit, Total Customers, Total Orders.

### 2. Table Visual
- Displays information in rows and columns.
- Helps users view detailed records.
- Example: Customer names, product names, sales amounts, and dates.

### 3. Matrix Visual
- Displays summarized data in a structured row-and-column format.
- Supports grouping and hierarchical analysis.
- Example: Compare sales by region and product category.

### 4. Bar Chart
- Displays horizontal bars to compare different categories.
- Useful for comparing product sales, employee performance, and regional sales.

### 5. Column Chart
- Displays vertical bars to compare numerical values.
- Example: Compare monthly sales or revenue across different products.

### 6. Line Chart
- Displays trends and changes over time.
- Useful for analyzing monthly sales, yearly revenue, and profit growth.
- Example: Understanding whether sales are increasing or decreasing over time.

### 7. Pie Chart and Donut Chart
- Display the contribution of different categories to a total.
- Useful for understanding percentage distribution.
- Example: Percentage of sales generated by each product category.

### 8. Scatter Chart
- Displays the relationship between two numerical variables.
- Helps identify patterns, clusters, and relationships.
- Example: Analyzing the relationship between sales and profit.

### 9. Map Visual
- Displays geographical information.
- Helps analyze business performance across different locations.
- Example: Comparing sales across cities, states, and countries.

## 3. What Are Slicers in Power BI?

A Slicer is an interactive filtering visual that allows users to filter dashboard data based on selected values.

**Example:**

Imagine a company has sales records from 2022 to 2026.

Using a Year Slicer, users can select 2025 and view only the sales information for that year.

Common Slicers:
- Year
- Month
- Region
- Product Category
- Customer Name

**Advantages of Slicers:**
- Make dashboards interactive.
- Help users explore specific information.
- Reduce the need to create separate reports.
- Allow quick comparisons between different categories.

## 4. Difference Between Chart and Slicer

| Chart | Slicer |
|---|---|
| Displays data visually. | Filters dashboard data. |
| Helps identify trends and patterns. | Helps users explore specific data. |
| Example: Bar Chart. | Example: Year Slicer. |

## 5. How Power BI Visualizations Work

The basic workflow of creating visualizations is:

1. **Data Source:** Import data from Excel, SQL Server, or other sources.
2. **Power Query:** Clean and transform the data.
3. **Data Modeling:** Create relationships between related tables.
4. **Visualizations:** Select suitable charts, cards, tables, and slicers.
5. **Dashboard:** Arrange visualizations to communicate meaningful business insights.

## 6. Practical Example – Sales Dashboard

Consider a company that wants to analyze its sales performance.

The following visuals can be created:

| Visualization | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Card – Total Sales | Displays overall sales revenue. |
| Card – Total Profit | Displays total profit. |
| Bar Chart – Sales by Product | Compares product sales. |
| Line Chart – Monthly Sales | Identifies monthly sales trends. |
| Pie Chart – Sales by Category | Shows category-wise sales contribution. |
| Slicer – Year | Filters sales data by year. |
| Table – Customer Details | Displays customer-level information. |

## 7. Importance of Power BI Visualizations

- Convert complex data into understandable information.
- Help identify business trends and patterns.
- Support faster decision-making.
- Make reports interactive and user-friendly.
- Help organizations monitor business performance.
- Improve communication of analytical results.
